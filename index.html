High concept · PC & web browser

Be the mad scientist of futuristic cooking.

Take orders, cook with lab-grade precision, and serve. Every gram, kelvin and second you get right or wrong shows up on the test subject who eats your dish: they glow, float, shrink or grow a giant head. Cooking Lab is closer to chemistry than to a kitchen, and it is always played for laughs.

Design pillars

Precision

Every step is measured

Weight, temperature, time, angle

Each step has a real target in real units (g, mL, K, °C, rpm, pH, mm). Precision is scored 0–100% and drives the outcome.

Mad science

Futuristic tools & elements

Cryo, plasma, quantum

Familiar gestures with impossible equipment: plasma butter, cryo-chives, helium flour, a centrifuge whisk. New gear unlocks new kinds of effects.

Consequences

See the result on the subject

Dosage → effect strength

Good dosing gives the requested effect. Sloppy dosing gives side effects. Bad dosing backfires: the subject turns green or shrinks. The payoff is visual and immediate.

Immersion

Realistic, step-by-step

Slice to cut, turn to stir

First-person bench with two hands. Mouse gestures copy the real motion: drag to slice, circle to stir, hold to pour, scroll to set heat.

Player promise & ethics

What the player feels

“I nailed 37.0 °C and the monkey's head doubled in size. Let's see what 40 does.” Curiosity first, then mastery.

Learning by playing

Units, conversions, Maillard reaction, emulsions, pH and phase changes are taught through the mechanics, not through text screens. A codex fills in with the real science behind each step.

No harm to test subjects

Subjects are volunteers on the lab's payroll. Effects are temporary, harmless and silly. Every subject leaves the bench happy, and a backfire costs you reputation, not the subject.

Progress & equipment

Serving earns lab credits and reputation. Credits buy stations (centrifuge, cryo chamber, plasma torch). Each station opens new recipes and new effects. See the Core loop tab.

Positioning

The cooking game where your mistakes are the content.

Hook 1 · Shareable transformations

Every served dish ends on a 3-second reaction shot (big head, glowing pig, floating monkey). One-click clip export for social.

Hook 2 · Real science, real units

Grams, kelvin, pH and rpm are the game's language. Players learn conversions and reactions without a single quiz.

Hook 3 · Instant play in the browser

No install. A link in a group chat or a class Discord gets someone cooking in under 30 seconds.

Ethics as a selling point

Subjects are happy volunteers and every effect wears off. Lab humor without cruelty keeps it safe for schools and streamers.

Targets & risks
  • PC build + WebGL build from one codebase
  • Browser memory budget: subject rigs use blendshapes, not unique meshes
  • Input latency must stay under 50 ms for slicing to feel fair
  • All recipes are data-driven (JSON) so designers can tune without code

For Creative Director · first-person bench

Camera & framing

Fixed first-person view over a steel bench, two gloved hands in frame. The order ticket pins to the top-right corner. Stations slide in from the sides.

Three words

Clean glass, goofy subjects, hot reactions. The lab is tidy and clinical; the chaos comes from the food and the subjects.

Feedback language

Liquids glow in reagent violet. Heat and success flashes use reaction orange. Measurement readouts always use a mono font, like lab equipment.

Audio

Glass clinks, Geiger-style ticks while dosing, a squeaky “boing” when an effect lands.

Effect vocabulary
  • Size: grow / shrink
  • Head: inflate / pinhead
  • Color: hue shift, sickly green on backfire
  • Glow, levitation, polka spots, big ears, clones
Rules for subjects

Big readable silhouettes, expressive faces, never in pain. A backfire reads as dizzy or embarrassed, not hurt.
